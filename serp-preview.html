FAQ

Common SERP Questions

Everything about title tags, meta descriptions, and optimising your click-through rate from Google.

What is the ideal title tag length?

Google typically displays title tags up to 580 pixels wide, which equates to roughly 50–60 characters. Titles longer than this get truncated with "..." in the search results. Shorter titles can be less descriptive, so aim for 55–60 characters as the sweet spot — front-load your most important keyword.

Does Google always use my meta description?

No — Google rewrites meta descriptions about 62% of the time according to Portent's research. It will replace your description with a snippet from the page that better matches the search query. This is why having good on-page content matters more than the meta description alone. Still, a well-written description improves CTR when Google does use it.

How do I get star ratings in my Google listing?

Star ratings appear through structured data (schema markup). Add AggregateRating schema to your product, service, or review pages. Use our Schema Markup Generator to create the JSON-LD code and paste it in your page's head section. Google must crawl and verify the ratings before they appear.

What makes a good meta description for CTR?

Write descriptions that answer the searcher's intent directly. Include your primary keyword naturally (Google bolds it), add a clear value proposition, and end with a soft call to action ("Learn more", "See all tools", "Try free"). Keep it 120–160 characters, make every word count, and avoid keyword stuffing.
